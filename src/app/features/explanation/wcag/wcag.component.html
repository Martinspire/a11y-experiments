WCAG

The Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) are a set of guidelines for making digital content accessible to people with disabilities. The guidelines are organized into four principles: perceivable, operable, understandable, and robust.

The WCAG guidelines are organized into three levels of conformance: A, AA, and AAA. Level A is the minimum level of conformance, while level AAA is the highest level of conformance.

The WCAG guidelines are designed to be technology-neutral, meaning that they can be applied to any type of digital content, including websites, mobile apps, and documents.

The WCAG guidelines are an important tool for ensuring that digital content is accessible to all users, including those with disabilities. By following the guidelines, developers can create content that is more inclusive and usable for everyone.

The WCAG guidelines are maintained by the World Wide Web Consortium (W3C), the organization that develops web standards. The guidelines are regularly updated to reflect changes in technology and best practices for accessibility.

The WCAG guidelines are an essential resource for anyone involved in the design, development, or testing of digital content. By following the guidelines, developers can create content that is more accessible, usable, and inclusive for all users.
